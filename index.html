    How the model works

    TMC runs language-training batches for corporate (B2B) clients. Each client pays a fee per learner for a whole course that can run over several months. The model works out each month's profit for every batch, and the YTD figures add up April to August. It answers one question: how much did each client and batch earn us after paying for the teaching and the operations team?

    Revenue for the month

    A course fee is spread evenly over the course's days. Each month's revenue is the share of days that fell in that month.

    Learners × Fee × (Days in month ÷ Course days)

    Less credit notes

    Refunds or reductions issued to a client (e.g. learners leaving a batch) are spread the same way and subtracted, giving net revenue.

    Teacher cost

    Course hours are spread over the course's days to get the hours taught in the month. Consultants are paid per hour. Full-time teachers are charged at their monthly salary ÷ available hours.

    Hours in month × Hourly rate

    Gross margin

    Net revenue minus teacher cost. This is the "Contribution" column on the Client Summary sheets.

    Ops team cost

    Operations salaries of the B2B team, less full-time teacher cost already charged, form a monthly pool. 40% is shared by the days each batch was active, 60% by learner-days (learners × active days).

    Pool × (40% × batch-days share + 60% × learner-days share)

    Contribution after ops

    What each batch leaves over to pay for sales, admin (G&A) and other overheads, and for profit.

    Worked example

    Operations cost pool, by month

    How the operations team cost to be shared was built each month.

    Because the pool is shared by active days and learner-days rather than revenue, small or low-fee batches carry a bigger ops cost per ₹ of revenue. A 1:1 batch running all month carries a full 40% "batch-day" share with one learner.

    Tie-out to the books (MIS)

    The model compared with TMC's MIS income statement, as on the MIS Reconciliation sheet. Revenue matches to the rupee. The cost differences are explained in the notes below the table.

    Consultant cost: the books include internal 1:1 sessions that are not charged to client batches, and payment timing differs month to month. Ops salaries: the books carry ₹38,000 a month more than the model. This is a staff cost outside the B2B business, so the model keeps it out of the B2B ops pool.

    Glossary

    YTD
    Year to date: April to August 2026, the first five months of FY 2026-27.
    Batch
    A group of learners taking one course together (e.g. A1.61 = level A1, batch 61). 1:1 batches have a single learner.
    Group / Non-group
    Group clients are Somika and Vinmart Foundation. All other corporate clients are Non-group.
    Gross revenue
    The part of the course fee earned in the period, before credit notes.
    Credit notes
    Reductions issued to the client, spread over the same days as the course.
    Net revenue
    Gross revenue less credit notes.
    Teacher cost
    Direct cost of the teacher's hours in the period (also called COGS).
    Gross margin %
    (Net revenue − Teacher cost) ÷ Net revenue.
    Ops team cost
    The batch's share of operations salaries (coordinators, recruiters, full-time teachers' unused time).
    Active days / learner-days
    Days the batch ran in the month, and those days × learners. Used to share the ops cost.
    Contribution margin %
    (Net revenue − Teacher cost − Ops team cost) ÷ Net revenue.
    Source: TMC YTD Profitability Analysis, FY 2026-27 (April–August 2026), B2B only: monthly Batch P&L and Revenue & COGS sheets, Payroll, YTD Summary and MIS Reconciliation. Figures are recalculated in the browser from batch-level monthly data and tie back to the workbook totals.
